About the project

The Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP) controller — a widely used trajectory-tracking algorithm in ROS 2's Nav2 stack — selects its lookahead distance using a simple velocity-based heuristic. This assumes the robot's pose is known perfectly, which breaks down as localization uncertainty (from noisy odometry, IMU drift, and sparse position corrections) grows over time.

I designed an SNMPC-RPP controller that replaces only the lookahead-selection module of RPP, leaving the rest of its architecture (velocity regulation, collision checking) untouched. At each 5 Hz control cycle, it predicts lateral and heading tracking error forward in time, propagates the robot's pose-error covariance through that prediction, and solves a constrained optimization (cost function + chance constraints + box constraints on lookahead) using the derivative-free COBYLA method to choose the optimal lookahead distance.

Validated in ROS 2 / Gazebo across three operating speeds (0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 m/s) on a rectangular reference circuit, the controller showed its biggest advantage exactly where it matters most: at higher speed, where uncertainty accumulates fastest, SNMPC-RPP reduced cross-track MAE by over 40% and RMSE by over 50% relative to baseline RPP, while keeping the rest of the robot's motion just as smooth.

About the project

This project tackles the classic inverted-pendulum balancing problem: keeping a two-wheeled robot upright while also controlling its position. I derived a linearized state-space model of the robot's position and chassis-tilt dynamics, then built and simulated it in MATLAB/Simulink with Simscape Multibody for realistic rigid-body physics.

I implemented both a PID controller and a Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) for the balancing task, then went a step further: rather than hand-tuning the LQR's Q and R weighting matrices, I implemented a Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm (MOGA) that searches for Pareto-optimal weightings by simultaneously minimizing the Integral Time Absolute Error (ITAE) for position and for chassis-tilt angle — two objectives that typically trade off against each other.

Comparing the GA-optimized LQR against manually tuned LQR and PID controllers in simulation, the GA-optimized controller reached faster position convergence while maintaining better chassis-tilt stability than either manual baseline — demonstrating that automated multi-objective tuning can outperform hand-tuning even on a well-understood classical control problem.
